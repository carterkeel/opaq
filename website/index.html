Your circle. Your map. No one else.

Share where you are with the people you choose. Every update is encrypted on your phone before it leaves, and only the phones in your circle can open it.

No accountYour phone makes its own key the first time you open Opaq. There's no email, phone number or password.
No server of oursUpdates travel through public Nostr relays as sealed messages, all the same size.
Open sourceEvery line of the app is public, so anyone can check these claims.

How an update travels

Your location goes from your phone to theirs through public relays that only ever hold a sealed message. Step through it, or play it.

Who can see what

        
Opaq encrypts everything about you, but your updates still pass through other people's computers. This is exactly what each of them can and can't see.

      

      

      

      

      

        

          
Share an area insteadChoose approximate location for any circle. It's applied before encryption, so even the people in that circle only see the area.

          
Connect through TorOn Android, with the Orbot app, relays see a Tor address instead of yours. If Orbot isn't running, Opaq doesn't connect at all.

          
Pick your relaysOpaq uses four public relays to start with. You can swap in ones you trust in Settings, under Connection.

        

        

          
          
          
        

      

    

  

  

    

      

        
Built for people who trust each other

        
Location sharing should be something everyone in the circle agreed to and can see happening.

      

      

        

          Every join is approvedInvites expire after an hour and work once, or for up to ten people. The circle's admin approves everyone who asks to join.
        

        

          Device codes you can compareEach phone has a 16-character code. Check it matches in person and that person gets a green check, so a swapped link can't fool you.
        

        

          Sharing is always visibleWhile you share, Android shows a "Sharing with" notification. Once a month Opaq shows who can see you and asks if that's still right.
        

        

          History that stays putEach phone keeps its own encrypted history, a week by default. It's never uploaded and never goes into backups.
        

        

          A PIN for the big thingsBackups, invites and other sensitive changes can ask for a PIN. Opening the app and looking at the map never do.
